# Clinical Triage Guidelines: Headache Evaluation & Triage

## Overview
Headaches can range from benign primary headache disorders (e.g., tension-type, migraine) to life-threatening secondary causes (e.g., subarachnoid hemorrhage, meningitis, temporal arteritis, intracranial mass).

## Red Flag Symptoms (Emergency - Immediate ER Evaluation)
- Sudden onset "thunderclap" headache (reaches peak intensity within seconds/minutes).
- Headache accompanied by fever, neck stiffness (nuchal rigidity), confusion, or altered mental status.
- New neurological deficits (e.g., focal weakness, numbness, speech difficulty, visual loss).
- New onset headache in patients over 50 years of age or patients with a history of cancer or immunosuppression.
- Headache following head trauma.

## Moderate Risk Symptoms (Urgent Physician Consultation within 24-48 Hours)
- Progressive worsening headache pattern over days or weeks.
- Migraine non-responsive to over-the-counter abortive treatment.
- Headache associated with nausea, mild photophobia, or sinus pressure.
- Unilateral headache with throbbing sensation disrupting daily tasks.

## Low Risk / Primary Headache (Self-Care & Routine Monitoring)
- Mild to moderate bilateral band-like tightness around the head (Tension headache).
- Headache associated with fatigue, stress, dehydration, or lack of sleep.
- Recommendation: Rest in a quiet dark room, hydration, and over-the-counter analgesics (e.g., Acetaminophen 500mg or Ibuprofen 400mg) as directed. Seek medical review if pain worsens or persists beyond 3 days.
