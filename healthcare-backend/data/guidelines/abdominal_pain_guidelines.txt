# Clinical Triage Guidelines: Abdominal Pain Evaluation & Triage

## Overview
Abdominal pain can stem from self-limiting gastrointestinal issues or life-threatening surgical emergencies (e.g., acute appendicitis, bowel perforation, ectopic pregnancy, aortic aneurysm).

## Red Flag Symptoms (Emergency - Immediate ER Evaluation)
- Sudden, severe, intolerable abdominal pain ("acute abdomen").
- Abdominal pain accompanied by rigid, board-like abdomen or severe tenderness to light touch.
- Vomiting blood (hematemesis) or passing black, tarry stools (melena).
- Pain accompanied by high fever, persistent vomiting, jaundice, or inability to pass gas/stool.
- Sharp right lower quadrant pain with rebound tenderness (suspicious for appendicitis).
- Sudden severe abdominal pain in pregnant individuals.

## Moderate Risk Symptoms (Urgent Medical Consultation within 12-24 Hours)
- Persistent localized abdominal pain lasting over 6 hours.
- Abdominal pain accompanied by low-grade fever, nausea, or diarrhea for more than 48 hours.
- Pain worsening with movement or coughing.
- Pain associated with unexplained weight loss or loss of appetite.

## Low Risk / Self-Care Guidelines
- Mild generalized crampy abdominal discomfort associated with indigestion, mild diarrhea, or constipation.
- Recommendation: Sip clear fluids, eat bland food (BRAT diet: bananas, rice, applesauce, toast), avoid dairy, fatty, or spicy foods. Use antacids for mild heartburn. Seek evaluation if pain persists beyond 24 hours.
